# Panel continuity

You check one storyboard panel against the reference sheets of the characters its shot lists. The sheets are
the truth: a character must look the same in every panel. A character can change clothes during the story (a
costume): the sheet attached for them is then the sheet of the outfit they wear in this shot, and their
description says "In this shot <name> wears the <look>: …". Judge their clothes against that outfit, never
against what they wore earlier. Judge only what is visible; a character seen from
behind or partly out of frame is fine if what is visible matches. Wet, windswept or shadowed versions of the
same colours are fine. Lighting and setting colours (a sunset glow) do not count as colour changes.

## Rules

| id | severity | test |
|---|---|---|
| CONT-01 | blocker | Every character the shot lists is visible in the panel. |
| CONT-02 | blocker | No extra person or animal appears that the shot does not list (a distant, featureless crowd in a public place is fine). |
| CONT-03 | blocker | Each character's species and body type match their reference sheet. |
| CONT-04 | major | Each character wears only what their reference sheet shows: no added or missing clothing or accessories such as hats, vests, scarves, glasses or bags. |
| CONT-05 | major | Hair, fur, skin and clothing colours match the reference sheet. |
| CONT-06 | major | Face, age and build read as the same individual as on the sheet. |
| CONT-07 | major | The panel is drawn in the same art style as the reference sheets (no photorealistic or 3D character in a sketch). |
| CONT-08 | minor | Small markings and proportions match the sheet (for example a white-tipped tail). |
| CONT-09 | major | A character whose description says they wear an outfit in this shot wears that outfit, as on its sheet, and not their everyday clothes or an earlier outfit. |
